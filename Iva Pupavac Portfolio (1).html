02 · Approach

How I work

The order rarely changes. Skipping the first step is how apps end up lecturing stressed parents.

01

Listen

Interviews, surveys, affinity maps. I collect the sentences people actually say, because those end up shaping the product more than any assumption I walk in with.

02

Find the person

Maya, Elizabeth, an expat with an empty flat. One specific human to design for keeps every decision honest.

03

Cut hard

MoSCoW until the first version fits in one sentence. What stays out matters as much as what goes in.

04

Sketch ugly

Paper first, in front of users the same day. Cheap mistakes now are the best investment I know.

05

Test with people

The back icon looked like a menu. Five users told me before launch did. That is what testing is for.

06

Finish properly

Style tile, components, a real prototype and a desirability test. Print taught me that finished means finished.

Principle one

Beauty is not optional

A screen that works but feels cheap loses trust before anyone reads a word. Craft is part of usability.

Principle two

Quotes over opinions

Mine included. When a user says “I hear enough of that elsewhere”, that sentence beats any idea I had in the shower.

Principle three

Systems, not screens

Atoms, tokens, components. If it only works once, it is not finished yet.

03 · About

From Novi Sad to here

The long way round, which turned out to be the right one.

I started where most designers pretend they didn’t: with deadlines, printers and clients who wanted it yesterday.

At FTN Novi Sad I studied Graphic Engineering and Design, which is a polite way of saying I learned colour profiles, paper weights and why a logo that fails at 16 pixels fails everywhere. I made 2D game art at EPIX, identities and logos at a Belgrade studio, and at Newpress in Smederevo I owned a magazine from the theme to the last page.

Then I moved to the Netherlands and chose to start again. The Ironhack UI/UX bootcamp gave my visual instinct a method: interviews, personas, testing, systems. It also gave me permission to be wrong early, which print never did.

What I bring now is the combination. I can run the research and I can make the result beautiful, and I don’t need to hand either part to someone else.
